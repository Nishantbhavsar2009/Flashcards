# Saisha’s NATA & Architecture Entrance Vocabulary Master Deck

> **Purpose:** A high-yield vocabulary and architecture-terminology deck for an Indian Class 12 student preparing for NATA and architecture admissions. It intentionally skips very basic school-level English and avoids filling the deck with rare “dictionary contest” words that are unlikely to help.

## Why this deck is relevant

- The current **NATA 2026** syllabus explicitly includes **Language Interpretation**: generating the meaning of words and sentences and understanding English grammar.
- NATA Part B also includes **General Knowledge, Architecture & Design**, **Visual Reasoning**, **Logical Derivation**, and **Design Sensitivity & Thinking**, including semantics, metaphors, context, observation, and analysis. That makes architecture/design vocabulary useful beyond pure synonym questions.
- **CEPT University B.Arch (2026–27)** uses NATA for B.Arch admission. For CEPT All-India seats, the published merit formula is 50% qualifying-exam aggregate + 50% valid NATA score.
- There is **no official fixed NATA vocabulary list**, so this is a curated master deck based on the abilities the syllabus actually tests, not a claim that these exact words will appear.

### Source basis (current when compiled)

- Council of Architecture, **NATA 2026 Information Brochure**, syllabus section: https://www.nata.in/
- CEPT University, **B.Arch Admission Information 2026**: https://cept.ac.in/programs/bachelors-in-architecture/admissions

## Flashcard schema

Every card is designed so the **front can show only the term**, while the **back** can reveal the remaining fields.

- **Part of speech / type**
- **Meaning** — exam-friendly, not dictionary-heavy
- **Synonyms / related terms**
- **Antonyms / contrast** when meaningful
- **Sentence** — usually architecture, design, reasoning, or exam context
- **Priority** — `Core`, `High`, or `Stretch`
- **Tags** — useful for filtering batches in a flashcard app

### Priority guide

- **Core:** Learn first. Most directly useful for NATA language interpretation, reasoning, and architecture vocabulary.
- **High:** Learn after Core. Strong value for tougher synonym/meaning/context questions and architecture awareness.
- **Stretch:** Useful for top-end vocabulary and design criticism; lower priority if time is short.

## Recommended study order

1. **Core Language Interpretation** cards
2. **Core Architecture & Design** cards
3. **Confusable Words**
4. **Idioms & Phrases**
5. **High** cards
6. **Stretch** cards

For spaced repetition, a sensible starting load is **15–25 new cards/day** plus all due reviews. For each card, try to recall the meaning **before** revealing the back, then make your own sentence once.

# Part IV — Commonly Confused Words

## C001 — affect vs effect
- **affect (verb):** to influence.

# Part VII — What NOT to over-study

- Do not spend weeks memorizing thousands of rare GRE-level words before mastering the **Core** deck.
- Do not treat architecture vocabulary as isolated trivia; connect each term to a sketch, building, or spatial example.
- Do not rely only on synonyms. NATA language interpretation can test meaning through **context, grammar, idiom, sequencing, and inference**.
- For an exam year after 2026, re-check the newest Council of Architecture brochure because the pattern and topic emphasis can change.
